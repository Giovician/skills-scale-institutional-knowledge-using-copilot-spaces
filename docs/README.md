## Project Management Process Summary

OctoAcme uses a customer-first, iterative lifecycle that begins with initiation and ends with continuous improvement. A new initiative starts by validating the business problem, confirming stakeholders, defining measurable outcomes, and deciding whether the work is ready to move forward. Planning then turns that approved idea into a clear backlog with prioritized work, estimated effort, acceptance criteria, milestones, dependencies, and a Definition of Done. This ensures that the team leaves the planning stage with a shared understanding of scope, ownership, and how success will be measured.

During execution, the team tracks work through a project board, keeps feedback loops active with standups and weekly delivery syncs, and uses structured pull-request practices to maintain quality and visibility. The project manager, product lead, and delivery team coordinate progress, risks, and blockers while using consistent communication patterns such as weekly status updates, demos, and escalation paths when issues affect delivery. The core roles described in the process docs help clarify ownership and reduce ambiguity across cross-functional work.

Quality is treated as a shared responsibility throughout the lifecycle. Acceptance criteria define what “done” looks like, CI enforces automated testing and security checks, and teams use manual QA and smoke tests for critical flows when needed. Before release, work must meet the required quality gates, including passing CI, validating smoke tests, and documenting rollback or mitigation plans. After a deployment or milestone, the team conducts retrospectives to capture improvements, assign action items, and measure whether those changes resolved the underlying issues.

Overall, OctoAcme’s project management approach emphasizes clarity, delivery discipline, cross-functional coordination, and measurable improvement. The process docs provide a lightweight framework for turning ideas into organized execution while keeping stakeholders informed and ensuring that teams learn from each release, milestone, and incident.
